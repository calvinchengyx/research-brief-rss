Research Brief Recipe

Ingredient 4

Data sources and tools

OpenAlex
The only search index. It supplies venues, author profiles, institutions, the papers themselves and most abstracts. Free, with an API key stored as a GitHub secret.
Semantic Scholar
Fills in abstracts that OpenAlex lacks, best effort.
Anthropic API
Runs the AI filter and writes the one-line summaries.
Brevo
Sends the email, one message per recipient.
GitHub Actions
Runs everything on a schedule and saves the issue, this page and the list of papers already sent back to the repository.
Editable files
config/journals.yaml, scholars.yaml, institutions.yaml, topics.yaml, recipients.yaml and settings.yaml.

Ingredient 5

AI model

Model
claude-haiku-5-5 (Claude Haiku 5.5), effort low, 20 papers per request.
What it decides
For each paper: keep or drop, which topic, relevance to the research interests from 0 to 10, and a one-sentence summary. It reads only the title, venue and abstract.
Cache
Each answer is saved in state/classified.json for 60 days, so a paper is never screened twice.
Editor's picks
The 4 shown papers with the highest relevance, if it is at least 6.
If the API fails
A keyword filter takes over and the email still goes out, without summaries.
Cost
About $0.15 to $0.25 per run.

The exact instructions the model receives

You screen new research papers for a weekly newsletter read by a computational social scientist.

Their research interests:
- online feminist discourse on Chinese social media platforms
- LLM-based content coding and annotation for social science
- gendered disinformation and narrative diffusion

Newsletter topics:
- css: Computational Social Science. Interdisciplinary journals and computational social science research.
- comm: Communication & Science Communication. Communication, media, journalism and science communication.
- cl: Computational Linguistics. LLMs, NLP and iSchool / information science venues, social-science topics only.

For each paper return:
- keep: false if it is not a research article or review (news, editorial, comment, correction, dataset, data or code deposit, supplementary material). For papers marked FILTER, also false unless it is about society, human behaviour, social media, communication, politics, psychology, science and the public, or the social uses and impacts of AI and language technology; false for pure natural science, medicine, mathematics, or purely technical ML/NLP work with no social-science angle. Otherwise true.
- topic: one of css, comm, cl. For preprints choose only from css, cl.
- relevance: 0-10, how close the paper is to the reader's research interests.
- summary: one plain sentence (max 25 words) on what the paper finds.

Papers marked FILTER come from wide venues, from coauthors or lab members, or are preprints. PREPRINT marks preprints.

Method

Workflow

Counts in the diagram are from the latest run.

How the 20 papers are chosen

Top priority
A followed scholar publishing in a followed venue. Always shown first.
Scholar watch
Followed scholars' work in other venues, including their preprints. At most 5.
Topics
The remaining places go to topics in the order CSS, COMM, CL. COMM gets 3 to 5 and CL gets 3 to 5, so the first topic cannot take every place.
Order within a group
Papers in followed venues come before preprints; followed scholars' preprints come before other preprints; then higher relevance first.
Never repeated
A paper sent once is never sent again. A preprint that later appears in a journal is shown again, labelled as now published.
Also this week
Up to 15 papers that missed the cut appear as one-line links at the end of the email.

Known limitations

  • Scholars are matched by name. OpenAlex sometimes merges two people with the same name, so a few followed profiles and their coauthor lists include strangers. Each entry flagged with namesakes is worth a look in state/following.md.
  • Coauthor lists can be broad: one large paper adds every author on it. Their papers still have to pass the social-science check.
  • The university list is roughly the QS top 100, typed from memory rather than copied from the official ranking.
  • Haiku is more lenient than Sonnet. For the same 10 days, Haiku kept 454 of 1,176 papers; Sonnet, run a few hours earlier, kept 335 of 1,132. A longer list of kept papers mostly lengthens the closest misses, since only 20 are shown.
  • Some papers have no abstract in OpenAlex or Semantic Scholar, so the model judges them on the title and venue alone.
  • OpenAlex indexes papers 1 to 5 days after publication. The 10-day window and the no-repeat rule cover that lag.
  • Relevance scores come from the model and are not calibrated; they decide order within a group, not whether a group gets places.
